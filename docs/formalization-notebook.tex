\section*{The exact target}
The uploaded manuscript defines
\[
h_\theta(r)=r\exp\!\left(-\bigl(\log(1/r)\bigr)^\theta\right)
\quad(0<r<1),\qquad h_\theta(0)=0.
\]
Its Theorem 1.1 asserts that, for a compact set $E\subseteq\mathbb R^2$,
\[
\frac23<\theta<1,\qquad \HH^{h_\theta}(E)>0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \LL^1(\Delta(E))>0.
\]
\[
\Delta(E)=\{\lVert x-y\rVert:x,y\in E\}.
\]
The formalization uses the Euclidean norm and Mathlib's arbitrary-gauge
Hausdorff measure constructor. For radii at least one, the gauge is extended
by the identity; this does not affect its small-radius Hausdorff measure.
The covering characterization has been proved:
\[
\HH^{h_\theta}(E)=
\sup_{\rho>0}\ \inf_{\substack{E\subseteq\bigcup_{n\ge0}U_n\\
\operatorname{diam}(U_n)\le\rho}}
\sum_{\substack{n\ge0\\U_n\ne\varnothing}}
h_\theta\bigl(\operatorname{diam}(U_n)\bigr).
\]
The independent \texttt{Challenge.lean} imports only Mathlib and repeats all
custom definitions. The solution does not import Challenge. The theorem
statement has no auxiliary Frostman, projection, or energy hypothesis.

\section*{The geometric foundation}
\begin{proposition}
For $0<r<1$, the gauge is positive and satisfies $h_\theta(r)\le r$.
Consequently $\HH^{h_\theta}(E)\le\HH^1(E)$, and countable sets have zero
gauge measure. Positive gauge measure supplies two distinct points of $E$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The exponential is strictly positive. The logarithm and its real power are
nonnegative, so the exponential is at most one. Mathlib's gauge comparison
theorem gives the measure inequality. Singletons are null for $\HH^1$,
hence also for the gauge measure; countable subadditivity gives the countable
case. A set containing at most one point is countable.
\end{proof}
The all-pairs distance map is continuous. It therefore maps the compact
product $E\times E$ to a compact, Lebesgue-measurable distance set. Two distinct
points give a positive distance; that fact alone does not give positive length.

\section*{The scalar summability foundation}
\begin{proposition}
For every $s,c>0$, one has $\log n\le c n^s$ for all sufficiently large
natural numbers $n$. For every $\theta,c>0$,
\[
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\exp(-c n^\theta)<\infty.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Mathlib proves $\log x=o(x^s)$ as $x\to\infty$ for $s>0$. Restricting its
eventual norm bound to natural numbers proves the first assertion. Applying
that assertion with coefficient $c/2$ gives
\[
\log n\le\frac c2 n^\theta
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\exp(-c n^\theta)\le\exp(-2\log n)=n^{-2}.
\]
The inverse-square series converges. The omitted finite initial segment has
no effect on summability, so comparison proves the claim.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}
The branch-counting exponent and filter-error power obey
\[
3\theta-2>0\ \Longleftrightarrow\ \theta>\frac23,
\qquad
\theta>\frac23\ \Longrightarrow\ -8(2\theta-1)<-1.
\]
Consequently, in the theorem's parameter range, every positive coefficient
eventually absorbs the logarithm into $n^{3\theta-2}$, and
\[
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}n^{-8(2\theta-1)}<\infty.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The exponent inequalities are linear arithmetic. Apply the logarithmic
domination result with exponent $3\theta-2$, and apply Mathlib's real-power
series criterion to the filter-error exponent.
\end{proof}

These results justify scalar convergence once suitable shell energies and
filter errors are supplied. They do not establish the geometric estimates
that produce those bounds.

\section*{The verified gauge Frostman construction}
\begin{proposition}
For $0<\theta\le1$ and a compact set $E$ of positive gauge measure, there are
a probability measure $\mu$ and a constant $C>0$ such that
\[
\mu(E)=1,\qquad \operatorname{supp}\mu\subseteq E,\qquad
\mu(\overline B(x,r))\le C h_\theta(r)\quad(0<r<1).
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Positive Hausdorff gauge measure gives positive gauge content. Finite dyadic
trees supply nonnegative weights with uniformly positive total mass and
saturated covering bounds. Choosing atoms on the compact set and normalizing
gives probability measures carried by $E$. These measures form a weakly
compact family, so a subsequence converges to a probability measure carried
by $E$. Portmanteau transfers open-ball bounds to the limit. Comparing an
arbitrary radius with consecutive dyadic scales, and applying the proved
gauge doubling estimate, yields the closed-ball bound. The probability bound
handles radii bounded away from zero.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}
The resulting measure has no atoms and has finite critical logarithmic energy
for every $\gamma\ge1$:
\[
J_\gamma(\mu)=\iint
\frac{(1+\log^+(1/\lVert x-y\rVert))^\gamma}{\lVert x-y\rVert}
\,d\mu(x)\,d\mu(y)<\infty.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The ball bound tends to zero with the radius, proving atomlessness. The
formal kernel is infinite on the diagonal. Decompose each pointwise
potential into dyadic annuli; the gauge ball bound controls its $n$-th
annular contribution by a constant times
\[
(n+1)^\gamma e^{-c n^\theta}.
\]
Logarithmic domination absorbs the polynomial into half the stretched
exponential. The resulting summable majorant uniformly bounds the potential.
Integrating against the finite measure proves the energy bound.
\end{proof}

The actual scalar Gaussian kernel is also controlled:
\[
K_\gamma(r)=\sum_{n\ge0}(n+1)^\gamma2^n e^{-4^n r^2}
\le C_\gamma\frac{(1+\log(1/r))^\gamma}{r}
\quad(0<r<1,\ \gamma\ge0).
\]
A split at the dyadic index nearest the inverse radius bounds the finite
prefix and compares the shifted tail with $K_\gamma(1)$. The measure-level
Fourier-energy bridge remains in progress.

\section*{The verified reconstruction bridges}
Dyadic frequency shells overlap at most three times. The quadratic L$^2$
bound proves convergence of square-summable shell pieces. Actual smooth
low-pass kernels preserve mass; their differences have uniformly bounded
L$^1$ norm and the precise dyadic Fourier support. Convolving removed
measures with these differences produces summable L$^1$ errors whenever
the removed masses are summable. Low-pass convolutions converge to the
original measure on Schwartz tests.

\begin{proposition}
Suppose a finite positive measure $\alpha$ satisfies
\[
\int\varphi\,d\alpha
=\int\varphi f\,dx+\int(\mathcal F^{-1}\varphi)g\,d\xi
\quad\text{for all Schwartz tests }\varphi,
\qquad f\in L^1,\quad g\in L^2.
\]
Then $\alpha$ is absolutely continuous with respect to Lebesgue measure.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let $d\sigma=|f|\,dx$. Cauchy--Schwarz and Plancherel give
\[
\left|\int\varphi\,d\alpha\right|
\le\int|\varphi|\,d\sigma+\lVert g\rVert_2\lVert\varphi\rVert_2.
\]
Use smooth compact cutoffs equal to one on a compact subset of an open set
$U$, zero off $U$, and bounded between zero and one. Their L$^2$ norm is at
most $|U|^{1/2}$. Inner regularity yields
\[
\alpha(U)\le\sigma(U)+\lVert g\rVert_2|U|^{1/2}.
\]
For a Lebesgue-null set, outer regularity provides open covers whose
Lebesgue and $\sigma$ masses both tend to zero. The bound forces its
$\alpha$ mass to be zero.
\end{proof}
Constructing the actual L$^2$ shell pieces and identifying the telescoped
series pairing is still in progress.
